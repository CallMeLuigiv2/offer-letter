# Proposal: "Explain it to me" videos (PRs, tickets, papers)

Status: **DRAFT 2026-10-21** — his answers so far: movie-quality look; wrong answer = a short scene of his
idea, then the teammate asks what went wrong and how he'd fix it; typing now, voice with v0.2; wants
"how the code works", "what's going wrong" and "how my plan works" videos in real-life terms. Open:
the review-stage rule (my take below) and the small questions at the bottom.
Research: `research/09-interactive-explainers.md` (recommends a browser-native player for speed; he chose
movie quality, so this proposal keeps Manim quality and gets interactivity from the player instead).

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

A technical problem is first shown as a real-life situation (a bakery that must send batches on a fixed
schedule even when the oven stalls; a post office; a restaurant pass). Characters (his teammates' portraits
with expressions and lip-flap) act it out on illustrated backgrounds, with Manim diagrams for the
technical side. At pause points he answers. In the "what's going wrong" video he says how he would solve
it *in real life*; his accepted answer becomes the Plan stage's **bridge** move (ARCHITECTURE §8.2), and
the plan "says it to a computer". A third video plays **his plan** back as the same story.

## 2. The four videos per ticket, and where his answers go

| Video | Unlocks | Chapters (60–90 s each, one idea, ends at a pause) | His responses | Where they go |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. How it works | review stage | the code's real behaviour, acted out — every behaviour in the PR gets equal airtime (decoys too), incl. the bad-day scenario (an engine pause) shown faithfully, never labelled right or wrong; each chapter names the function it depicts | click predictions ("the oven stalls 1 s — what happens to the 3 batches due?"), an occasional open "what would the customer see?", and a **"that looks wrong" flag** on any moment | flags become draft notes beside the diff (he still finds the line and writes the comment) |
| 2. What went wrong (+ the asks) | after the review is submitted | one chapter per issue (missed ones first) and one per requester ask: real-life failure → zoom into exactly where in the code | open: "in the bakery, how would you fix this?" → checked → if it wouldn't work: a short scene of his idea playing out → teammate in the chat bar asks what went wrong and how he'd fix it → continue | his accepted real-world solutions + the story fill the Plan's **bridge** move (story, solution, mapping seeds) |
| 3. Your plan, played out | plan stage, after Contracts | his steps acted out with his example's values; the surprise example too | "is this what you meant?" (yes/no/partly), predict the next value | the plan review sees the answers; mismatches become notes |
| 4. The paper in real life | experiment tickets (later) | claim → why it matters → how it works (analogy) → the key number → assumptions and limits (Keshav's passes) | "how would you test this claim on our system?" | the experiment plan's hypothesis / tolerance |

Every chapter has one-click "replay this chapter" (learners seek back from a question, Kovacs 2016) and
subtitles. Questions interrupt every 60–90 s (interpolated questions roughly halve mind-wandering,
Szpunar 2013). Mostly clicks; about one in three pauses is an open answer (research/09 §1, §5 of 07).

## 3. Review-stage rule (my recommendation, waiting on his OK)

A video that *says* what's wrong before he submits turns the review into "find the line for a bug I was
told about"; noticing problems nobody pointed out is the skill the job tests most (research/01). Video 1
gives most of the clarity he asked for without the giveaway: it shows what the code *actually does* in the
story, bad scenario included, so if batches get mis-timed he sees it happen; deciding that it's wrong and
finding the line stays his. Because every behaviour gets a chapter and each chapter names its function,
"where in the codebase" is answered for everything equally, so it doesn't point at the bugs. Video 2
(after submitting) shows each problem and the exact place in the code.

## 4. How it's built (movie quality + interactive)

- **Script JSON** (research/09 §9 shape: beats → segments → questions with rubric, accepted alternatives,
  common wrong ideas each with a `try_idea` branch, hint ladder; every factual claim cites a fact extracted
  from the diff/AST/paper). Hand-written for KLN-01 first (research/09 rec. 9), generated by an agent later.
- **Deterministic Manim renderer** (`tools/explainer/`, built from the fundamentals-studio widgets): turns a
  script into chapter MP4s + subtitles. The LLM never writes Manim code (that's where on-demand Manim fails:
  hallucinated APIs, layout, research/09 §6b); it writes schema-checked data, the renderer is ours and
  tested. New "story" widgets: `Stage` (background + character slots), `Character` (portrait, expression,
  lip-flap from the Kokoro audio envelope), `Prop` (batches, trays, letters as moving objects), plus the
  studio's code panel, queues, timelines.
- **Branches**: each anticipated wrong idea is pre-rendered as a short clip. An idea no branch covers skips
  the scene and goes straight to the teammate chat (a parameterised "try it" simulator per concept comes
  later).
- **Video 3** (his plan) is rendered on demand from his plan JSON with a fixed template (no LLM code), at
  preview quality in the background while he keeps planning ("your plan video is rendering").
- **Player** (web): plays chapter clips, pauses at questions, chat bar for open answers, branch clips,
  teammate portrait in the chat, replay chapter, "show me" escape after the teammate round (marked
  "seen, not solved" and brought back in the debrief).
- **Checking answers**: a separate grader call (Haiku, rubric + accepted alternatives + common wrong
  ideas, must quote his words; the server checks the quote) returns works / different-but-valid / partly /
  off-track / can't-tell; the teammate persona only talks. Leak check: the generator never sees the answer
  key; review-stage scripts are compared against it server-side (line ranges, token overlap, a Haiku judge
  that sees only the key and the narration).
- **Assets**: backgrounds generated locally with ComfyUI + Animagine XL 4.0 (installed, D4) in the
  portraits' style; the pixel office for office scenes; Kokoro voices (cast after his blind test, D5).

## 5. Build order (after the Plan stage lands)

1. Renderer core + 3 story widgets + one hand-written KLN-01 "How it works" chapter, end to end with one
   click question. (Ship gate: one chapter plays, pauses, a question gates it.)
2. Player in the app (chapters, questions, replay, flags → review notes).
3. KLN-01 video 2 with open answers, grader, try-your-idea branches, teammate chat, bridge hand-off.
4. Video 3 (plan played out) template.
5. Script generator for new tickets (facts + leak checks), then papers, then voice (v0.2).
